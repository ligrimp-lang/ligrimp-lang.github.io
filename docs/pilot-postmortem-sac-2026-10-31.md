# The SAC-rate episode: what went wrong, and the redo (2026-10-31)

Rustam watched the published SAC-rate episode on YouTube and Instagram on 2026-10-31 and judged it bad in content, facts and
visuals: the cat had no business in an episode about the SAC rate (nobody asked for it; it came from the 28 October rule for the
cat episode and was applied without a question), the facts must follow the GUE Fundamentals material his apps' expert reviewers
read (the pilot skipped that step), and the material is not interesting: one view, no likes, no comments, and not something he
would send a friend. His order: analyse, redo, he reviews on his laptop, and only after his word is it replaced on YouTube and
Instagram. His GUE reviewer then read the published words against the Fundamentals and Tech 1 workbooks, and the redo is built on
those findings. This document is the analysis, the research, the concepts, the chosen one, the new words, and the list of every claim
in them that the Mac checks against the GUE Fundamentals workbook before anything is rendered.

The one-view count says little by itself: it was posted to a channel and a profile with no followers, so the sample is one
person. The faults below stand without it.

## The episode as published

Eight segments in about fifty seconds, from `EPISODES["sac-rate"]` as it was: a title card over the dolphin clip ("What is your
SAC rate?"), then five cards over the blurred reef loop, three of them with the swimming cat, one with the ascending cat and one
with its head, each carrying a line of arithmetic ("120 × 12 = 1,440 L ÷ 40 min = 36 L/min"; "14 ÷ 10 + 1 = 2.4 bar · 36 ÷ 2.4 =
15"), then the Dive log statistics screen for one sentence, then the outro. The voice read the arithmetic aloud.

## What went wrong

**The hook.** The first two seconds are a definition over footage: "Every gas plan starts with your SAC rate: what you breathe in
a minute, at the surface." Nothing is at stake, nobody is in it, and the title presumes the viewer already knows what a SAC rate
is and cares. A diver scrolling past has no reason to stay; the title is not in the words a diver uses either (they type "why do I
use so much air", not "SAC rate").

**The facts and the wording.**
- The term is used loosely. The episode calls a litres-per-minute figure "SAC rate" without saying which convention it follows:
  much training material uses SAC for a pressure rate (bar a minute, which depends on the cylinder) and RMV for the volume rate;
  GUE's own term and unit are what the apps' reviewers go by, and the pilot never checked them. Dive log's own statistics caption
  says "surface consumption rate".
- "Litres a minute, at depth" (a card title) was wrong wording for 36 L/min: that figure is surface litres from the cylinder per
  minute, before the pressure correction, not what is breathed at depth. The review had already said "from the cylinder".
- "120 bar × 12 L = 1,440 L" was stated flat; gas compressibility makes it "about", as the review had also said.
- Numbers were thrown at the viewer as arithmetic: the voice read "a hundred and twenty bar times twelve litres: fourteen hundred and
  forty litres", the exact fault Rustam named on 28 October, with no situation the numbers belonged to.
- "Do it for a few dives and use the average" is thin: it says nothing about what moves the rate (cold, workload, stress, weighting)
  which is the part a diver can act on and the part GUE teaches.
- The cat. Three poses of a mascot in an episode about a breathing rate, and a thumbnail line ("the C in C × A × T") that only means
  something to someone who saw the other episode.

**The pacing and the visuals.** Five of eight segments are formula cards on a dark blur; the only movement is the bubbles from the
cat's mouth. There is no dive, no diver, no moment, and the app is on screen for four seconds at the end. It reads as a worked
homework problem recorded over a screensaver, and it gives no reason to be a video rather than a page.

**Why a diver would neither like nor share it.** There is no surprise and no payoff: nothing to argue with, nothing to recognise,
nothing to measure yourself against. Divers share two things about air: a situation they have lived ("my buddy always has more") and
a number they can compare themselves to. The episode has neither. The cat makes it look generated, and the arithmetic makes it look
like school.

So: a situation in the first two seconds, one number the viewer can hold against their own, one worked example, the trend as the
payoff, the app as the tool, under a minute, big type that is a figure and not a formula.

## What the GUE expert found in the published words (2026-10-31)

Rustam's GUE reviewer read the published script against the Fundamentals and Tech 1 workbooks. The findings, which the redo is
built on (page references are the reviewer's):

1. **The term.** GUE calls it SCR, surface consumption rate: the volume of gas consumed in one minute while at the surface
   (Fundamentals Workbook p. 27). The 2010 Tech 1 manual says it is usually called SAC or RMV (p. 121). Dive log's own screen says
   SCR. Rustam's decision: SCR, GUE's term; the first time it appears it is given in full and explained in plain words, with "also
   known as SAC", because the term is not common outside GUE; after that "SCR" or "the rate", and neither the title nor the opening
   card relies on the abbreviation.
2. **The method, in GUE's order.** The litres used (bar used × cylinder litres); then the rate at depth, DCR, as litres ÷ minutes; then
   divided by the pressure at the average depth in ATA (Fundamentals pp. 27–28; Tech 1 Workbook p. 24).
3. **"Do it for a few dives and use the average" was misleading.** GUE: measure over a range of dives in realistic conditions, easy and
   hard, plan with the higher figure, rounded up, and with the highest rate in the team (Tech 1 Workbook p. 24).
4. **"Every gas plan starts with your SAC rate" was misleading.** A GUE gas plan starts with minimum gas (Tech 1 Workbook p. 23), which
   uses a stressed rate of 20 L/min × 2 divers, not the diver's own rate (Fundamentals p. 31).
5. **Missing.** GUE's planning figure of 20 L/min (Fundamentals p. 28), and that 18 is rounded up to 20 (Tech 1 Workbook p. 24). In the
   published video a relaxed 15 L/min was the only number a viewer took away.
6. **"And draws it over time" goes.** Statistics is not in the Dive log build up for review, so the episode claims nothing about it, and
   shows the dive page rather than the statistics screen.

## Three concepts

**A. The buddy with more gas.** Same dive, same forty minutes; you surface with fifty bar, your buddy with a hundred. One number
explains it. The number, explained in plain words; how to get yours from the last dive, in GUE's order; GUE's planning figure and the
round-up; where a gas plan really starts; Dive log on the dive page.

**B. Your computer knows your breathing better than you do.** A surprising claim first: the average depth and the time are already
logged, and the rate is one division away; ten dives later the trend says whether you are getting calmer. Ends on the statistics screen.

**C. The most expensive sentence in diving: "I'm fine on air".** A diver who has never measured the rate cannot plan a dive; the
rate is the first number of every plan. Risk: it lectures, and it opens on an accusation.

**The pick is A.** It opens on a situation every diver has lived, it promises a payoff in the first sentence and delivers it (the
number, in plain words), the worked example is the viewer's own last dive rather than a classroom one, and the ending gives the
viewer GUE's planning figure and the right place for a gas plan to start, instead of a relaxed 15 L/min to feel good about. B is a good second episode once the term is established; C is the tone Rustam rejected.

## Rustam's structure for the redo (2026-10-31)

His words: the problem statement is that the number is needed to plan dives and reserves and is a key component of gas planning, so
the episode starts by emphasising why the number matters; then, step by step, friendly and easy, how anyone with little background
gets it without complex maths; and at the very end, briefly, that some dive computers track it too and that for Shearwater divers
Dive log shows the rate per dive and how it changes over time. Under sixty seconds, no cat. The reviewer's finding 4 applies to the
first part: the words do not say that every gas plan starts with the rate (in GUE, minimum gas comes first, with a stressed rate).

## What changes in how episodes are made

- No mascot in an episode unless Rustam asks for one; the cat is per episode and off here.
- The GUE check is a step before the render, not after the publish: every claim and figure goes on a numbered list, the Mac checks it
  against the workbooks, and the entry is corrected first. The published episode's review is the model: six findings, each with a
  page.
- He reads the words before anything is rendered, as the 28 October rule already said; this redo was written for that read.
- The title is the diver's question in the diver's words, not the term; a term the diver may not know is given in full and explained
  the first time it is said.
- No claim about an app feature that is not in the build under review.
- After publishing, the retention curve in YouTube Studio and the view rate in Instagram Insights are read at the first three
  seconds, and the next episode's hook is judged by them, not by the view count of a channel with no followers.

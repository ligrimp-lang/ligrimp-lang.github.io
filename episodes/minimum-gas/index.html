Minimum gas, step by step

Rustam Ismailov, 1 November 2026 · an episode of about three minutes

Minimum gas is the gas you must always keep in your tank. It is the gas two divers need to ascend from the deepest point to the surface on one tank, when one of them is out of gas and both are under stress. Courses use different values. We calculate it with a cat, C × A × T: the consumption of both divers, times the average pressure during the ascent, times the time to ascend. This episode calculates it step by step on one example dive and ends with a rounded table by the deepest point.

What is said

Minimum gas is the gas you must always keep in your tank. Imagine that, at the deepest point, your buddy signals that they are out of gas. You share your gas, and you both ascend. How much gas must you keep in your tank for this?

Minimum gas is the gas two divers need to ascend from the deepest point to the surface, on one tank, under stress. Courses use different values. We calculate it with a cat: C times A times T, for two divers. C is the consumption of both divers. A is the average pressure during the ascent. T is the time to ascend.

For example, in our dive the deepest point is twenty metres, and two stressed divers breathe twenty litres per minute each from one eleven point one litre tank. They take one minute to solve the problem, then ascend at three metres per minute on average. Step one: let's calculate C, the consumption of both divers. Twenty litres per minute each, times two divers, is forty litres per minute. Step two: let's calculate A, the average pressure during the ascent. Take three atmospheres at twenty metres and one atmosphere at the surface, add them, then divide by two: two atmospheres.

Step three: let's calculate T, the time to ascend. Twenty metres divided by three metres per minute, plus one minute to solve the problem, is seven point seven minutes, rounded up to eight minutes. Step four: let's calculate the gas for both divers, C times A times T. Forty litres per minute times two atmospheres times eight minutes is six hundred and forty litres.

Step five: let's calculate the same gas in bar. Six hundred and forty litres divided by the tank's volume, eleven point one litres, is fifty-seven point seven bar, rounded up to sixty bar. That is minimum gas for this dive.

Your plan must never use minimum gas. When either gauge reaches it, the dive is over and you both ascend, even if the plan says otherwise. For a single aluminium eighty, minimum gas is forty bar for a deepest point of ten metres, sixty bar for twenty metres, and one hundred bar for thirty metres. It is never less than forty bar. Between two depths, use the deeper one. Deeper than thirty metres, calculate it.

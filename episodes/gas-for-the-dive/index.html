How much gas does your dive need?

Rustam Ismailov, 29 October 2026 · an episode of under a minute

Divers work out the gas a dive needs with a cat: C × A × T. C is your consumption, A is the pressure at your average depth, T is the time. Multiply them and you have litres; divide by the size of your cylinder and you have bar, which is what your gauge reads. This episode does it once, for a made-up dive, in under a minute.

What is said

You are planning a dive, say forty minutes at around twenty metres. Will the gas on your back cover it? Divers work that out with a cat.

C is your consumption, your SAC rate: what you breathe in a minute at the surface. Say fifteen litres a minute. A is the pressure at your average depth: one bar at the surface, plus one for every ten metres. Twenty metres: three bar. T is the time of the dive: forty minutes.

Fifteen, times three, times forty: eighteen hundred litres. Your gauge reads bar, so divide by the size of your cylinder. Twelve litres: a hundred and fifty bar. That is the dive. Your minimum gas comes on top.

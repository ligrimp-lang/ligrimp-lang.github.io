What is said

To plan your dives and your minimum gas, you need one number: your surface consumption rate, SCR. It is a key part of gas planning.

SCR is the litres you breathe per minute at the surface. It is also called SAC, surface air consumption. But we do not always breathe air, because the gas can be nitrox or trimix.

For example, in our dive the average depth is twenty metres, and the dive lasts thirty-five minutes. The tank holds eleven point one litres, and its pressure went from two hundred bar to sixty bar. Step one: let's calculate the gas you used, in bar. Two hundred bar minus sixty bar is one hundred and forty bar. Step two: let's calculate the same gas in litres. One hundred and forty bar times the tank's volume, eleven point one litres, is one thousand five hundred and fifty-four litres.

Step three: let's calculate the gas you used per minute, at depth. One thousand five hundred and fifty-four litres divided by thirty-five minutes is forty-four point four litres per minute. Step four: let's calculate the average pressure. Twenty metres divided by ten, plus one, is three atmospheres. Step five: let's calculate your SCR, the rate at the surface. Forty-four point four litres per minute divided by three atmospheres is fourteen point eight, rounded up to fifteen litres per minute.

Collect your own SCR over several dives, in different conditions, and plan with a high value, not the average, rounded up. Until you have measured your own SCR, start with twenty litres per minute. This table shows the bar you use every five minutes. If you find your consumption is higher than twenty litres per minute, plan with your own number.

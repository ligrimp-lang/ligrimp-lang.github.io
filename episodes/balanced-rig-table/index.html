How much weight do you need for the dive?

Rustam Ismailov, 10 November 2026 · a balanced rig, calculated on paper, in under three minutes

The answer comes from a balanced rig. A rig is balanced when it passes two checks, one at the end of the dive and one at the start. Write every part of your rig in a table, and both checks become simple sums.

The two checks

At the endAt the 3 m stop, with 50 bar left and an empty wing, you stay without effort.
At the startAt the deepest point, with a full tank, you can ascend by swimming if the wing fails.

Our example

Diver80 kg
Tankone aluminium tank, 11.1 L, on air
Suit5 mm wetsuit
Watersalt water
Deepest point30 m, with a full tank of 200 bar

The table

Each part in kilograms in the water: plus means it floats, minus means it sinks.

Our example, part by part, in kg in the water
PartEnd: 3 m, 50 bar, empty wingStart: 30 m, 200 bar, wing failed
Wetsuit+3.9+1.3
Air in the tank−0.7−2.7
Empty tank+2.0+2.0
Regulator−1.4−1.4
Plate, harness, wing, fins, small items−1.8−1.8
Body (our diver, an estimate)+1.6+1.6
Lead, 4.0 kg−3.6−3.6
Total0 kg, neutral−4.6 kg, heavy

Step by step

  1. The end of the dive

    +3.9 − 0.7 + 2.0 − 1.4 − 1.8 + 1.6 − 3.6 = 0 kg, neutral

    Write each part at the 3 m stop, then add lead until the column adds up to zero. Our diver needs 4.0 kg of lead, which weighs about 3.6 kg in water. The body value is only an estimate: every body is different, so the lead is found by the check in the water.

  2. The start of the dive

    +1.3 − 2.7 + 2.0 − 1.4 − 1.8 + 1.6 − 3.6 = −4.6 kg, heavy

    At 30 m the wetsuit is compressed and lifts only about 1.3 kg, and the full tank holds about 2.7 kg of air. The tank, the regulator, the plate, the body and the lead do not change with depth, so they are copied across.

The rule

At the start, you must be able to ascend by swimming if the wing fails. Most divers can do it with a rig about 5 kg heavy, so on paper our rig passes, but only just. Test your own limit, because your own check in the water decides. If your rig is too heavy, change the gear or carry part of the lead where you can drop it.

Now, your own numbers

At the end of your next dive, do the weight check at the 3 m stop, with 50 bar left and an empty wing, and update your table. Keep a record of every check, and over time you will see how your weighting changes with your equipment.

Transcript

How much weight do you need for the dive? The answer comes from a balanced rig. A rig is balanced when it passes two checks. At the end, you stay at the last stop without effort. At the start, you can ascend by swimming if the wing fails.

For example, our diver weighs eighty kilograms and dives with a single eleven point one litre aluminium tank and a five millimetre wetsuit, in salt water. Plus means it floats, and minus means it sinks.

First, the end of the dive. At the three metre stop, with an empty wing and fifty bar left in the tank, you must stay there without effort. The wetsuit lifts about three point nine kilograms. The air left in the tank weighs about zero point seven kilograms. The empty aluminium tank floats: it lifts about two kilograms. The regulator weighs about one point four kilograms in water, and the plate, the harness, the wing, the fins and the small items about one point eight kilograms. Our diver's body lifts about one point six kilograms, but this is only an estimate, and every body is different. Now we add lead until this column adds up to zero kilograms. Four kilograms of lead weighs about three point six kilograms in water, and our rig is neutral.

Then the start: at thirty metres with two hundred bar, you must be able to ascend by swimming if the wing fails. The wetsuit is compressed, so it lifts only about one point three kilograms. The air in the full tank weighs about two point seven kilograms. The tank, the regulator, the plate, our diver's body and the lead do not change with depth, so we copy them across. So at the start, our rig is about four point six kilograms heavy.

Most divers can ascend by swimming with a rig about five kilograms heavy, so on paper our rig passes, but only just. Test your own limit, because your own check in the water decides. If your rig is too heavy, change the gear or carry part of the lead where you can drop it.

Now use your own numbers. At the end of your next dive, do the weight check at the three metre stop, with fifty bar left and an empty wing, and update your table. Keep a record of every check, and over time you will see how your weighting changes with your equipment.
